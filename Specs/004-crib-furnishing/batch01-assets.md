## Placement Notes

- **Floor height is measured, not assumed.** The lounge collision floor sits at
  Z 10.49-10.53, not the Z ~6 the approval board projected. The suggested Z column
  is `10.5 - mesh bounding-box minimum`, which rests the lowest geometry on that
  floor. Easiest alternative: drop the actor in and press **End** to snap it down,
  now that `Plane_049` has working collision.
- The dining zone is on the upper Z ~462 band. Re-measure there before trusting a
  number; do not reuse the lounge Z.
- Set each actor to **Static** mobility so it joins the existing bake.
- **Rug01** has no simple collision. Its mesh is 6.8 mm thick so it cannot obstruct
  the pawn, but set the actor's collision preset to NoCollision if you want it
  excluded from queries entirely. Its source is 6.85 x 7.89 m: scale it to the
  seating group rather than placing it 1:1.
- **FloorLamp01** is decorative only. No light component is attached, and its
  emissive map is deliberately not connected until the separate lighting check.
- **HousePlant04 measures 41 x 48 x 27 cm**, so it is a tabletop plant rather than
  the floor-standing corner plant the schedule implies. `banana_plant01` and
  `house_plant01/02/03/05` are available if a floor plant is wanted.
- **Rug01 is the plain linen variant.** The source atlas holds four rugs and
  `rug02`-`rug04` exist; say the word and one can be imported instead.
- Wall art (WMCYN pattern and chrome wordmark) is not in this import. It needs the
  framed-art treatment from the LOGOS source and is still outstanding.

## Lime Shag Rug (upper lounge)

`SM_Crib_Rug2` (`StaticMeshActor_28`, upper floor at `-860, -450, 470`, scale 0.75)
now renders as a lime green shag pile. Its transform is unchanged.

The swap is a **component material override on that one actor**. Both rug actors
share `SM_Crib_Rug01` and take their material from the mesh, so editing
`M_Crib_Rug` would have recoloured the ground-floor rug (`StaticMeshActor_37`)
too. That actor and the mesh asset are untouched.

- `M_Crib_Rug_LimeShag` - opaque, three texture samples, no translucency or
  dynamic shadowing, so it stays inside the Quest budget.
- `T_Crib_RugShag_Fibers` (1K, TC_Masks, linear) packs `R` strand height,
  `G` tuft height, `B` dye variation. `T_Crib_RugShag_Normal` (1K, TC_Normalmap)
  is baked from the same height field and, like the project's other normal maps,
  is OpenGL-handed and imports with Flip Green enabled.
- Both maps are generated by `Tools/Materials/make_shag_textures.py`,
  which is dependency-free: neither the system Python nor Unreal's has numpy or
  Pillow. Re-run it to change fibre length, density or flow, then reimport.
- Fibre depth is **shading, not geometry**. The mesh is a 40-triangle plane, so
  the pile comes from a bump-offset height field, a dense strand normal, AO in
  the tuft valleys and a three-tone lime ramp. It reads as plush from standing
  height and holds up close; what it cannot do is break the rug's silhouette,
  so the edge is still a flat 5 mm lip. Real pile depth would need a new mesh
  and is not worth the Quest cost.
- Every look control is a material parameter in group `Shag`, so tuning is a
  material instance, not a graph edit: `FiberTiling` 13, `PileDepth` 0.05,
  `FiberBase`/`FiberBody`/`FiberTip`, `DyeMin` 0.96, `DyeMax` 1.05,
  `AOFloor` 0.66, `RoughBase` 0.94, `RoughTip` 0.76, `FiberSpecular` 0.2.
- Colours are authored in sRGB and converted to linear **once** before being
  written to the vector parameters, which the shader reads as linear. Do not
  re-apply gamma when retuning; the generator script prints both forms.
- `FiberTiling` was cut from 24 to 13 because the 1K maps moired against the
  mip chain at mid distance. Raise it only if the moire is checked again.

## Abbotsford Couch (upper lounge, alcove wall)

`SM_Crib_CouchAbbotsford` stands against the plaster alcove wall the editor was
facing, centred on the rug. It models the user's own couch, photographed in
`IMG_1557`-`IMG_1562`: a black 3-piece modular on a pale ply plinth, with the
WMCYN banner above it and the lime shag rug in front.

Dimensions are the Freedom **Abbotsford Fabric Modular Sofa, 4.5 Seater
(3 Piece), Mornington Midnight** (product 24960076), measured in the editor at
**110 deep x 324 wide x 87 high cm**, seat top at 45 cm, matching the product's
stated 324 x 110 x 87 and 45 cm seat height.

Placement, all world-space:
- Actor origin `-1224, -481, 473.5`, no rotation, unit scale.
- Back face at X `-1279`, 3 cm clear of the alcove wall plane at X `≈ -1282`.
- Floor at Z `470`; alcove clear width `≈ 490 cm` centred on Y `≈ -482`, so the
  couch leaves roughly 83 cm either side.

How the wall was measured, because the obvious routes do not work here: the Crib
shell is one imported FBX blueprint (`FbxScene_wmcyn_crib_default_unity_transfer`)
whose actor bounds span the whole map, and **none of its geometry has collision**,
so `trace_world` and `ScreenCoordsToWorld` both return nothing against it. The
numbers above come from a top-down `CaptureViewport` with the annotation grid at
the floor height, reading the wall line off the labelled 50 cm grid. Re-measure
the same way rather than trusting a trace.

Construction and limits:
- Three assets, three actors: `SM_Crib_CouchAbbotsford` plus two instances of
  `SM_Crib_CouchPillow` (`_L`, `_R`). The pillows are deliberately **separate**
  so they can be moved, swapped or removed without touching the couch.
- Both meshes are authored by `Tools/Models/make_couch_obj.py`, which writes OBJ
  that `StaticMeshTools.import_file` brings in 1:1, `usemtl` names surviving as
  the slot names. Couch 9,880 tris, pillow 1,020.
- Every piece is a superellipsoid. Its exponent is the one knob for how square a
  form is: 1.0 is an ellipsoid, 0 is a box. The couch sits at 0.13-0.19 and the
  pillows at 0.17 - square slabs with a small soft radius, matching the real
  furniture. An earlier pass at 0.34-0.48 read as pillowy and cartoonish.
- Dropping the exponent alone is not enough. At low values nearly the whole
  angular range maps onto the flat faces and the rounded edge is squeezed into a
  few degrees, so uniform sampling gives it one or two segments and it reads as
  a chamfer. `cluster()` remaps each quadrant so vertices bunch at its midpoint,
  which is where the edge is. That is why `nu` must divide by 4 and `nv` by 2.
  Cushions are cut 5 cm wider than their share of the width so neighbours press
  into a crease; butting them at true size left open slots.
- Against the blocky, cartoon read, three things: per-vertex displacement by two
  octaves of value noise, a per-cushion seed plus small rotation jitter so no two
  are identical, and a sag term that dips the seat tops where people sit. The
  mesh therefore measures 111.3 x 324.7 x 87.7 rather than a clean 110 x 324 x 87
  - the overshoot is the irregularity and is intended.
- UVs are emitted in centimetres over a 28 cm tile, each vertex projecting onto
  the plane its normal most faces. Without that, a 324 cm base and a 15 cm
  cushion got the same 0-1 range and the weave changed size across the couch.

Materials:
- `M_Crib_CouchFabric` now samples a real base colour and normal from
  `Tools/Materials/make_couch_textures.py`: a plain weave plus creases built from
  ridged value noise with a warped domain, which gives organic lines that do not
  look tiled. Roughness varies from worn patches; specular 0.14.
- An earlier pass left the body untextured, reasoning that weave is invisible on
  black and costs a sample. That was the wrong trade for a scan-style target.
- The first textured pass put too much of the crease into the **albedo** and the
  fabric read as printed damask. Creases belong almost entirely in the normal
  map; the albedo keeps only about a third of the contrast it had.
- `M_Crib_CouchPillow` uses a charcoal ground with light crosshatch threads. It
  started as mid-grey, which lit far brighter than the couch and read as a white
  pillow with dark lines, the inverse of the reference.

Honest limit: this is procedurally authored geometry dressed to read as scanned,
not a scan. The silhouette, sag and surface are plausible but they are invented,
not measured off the real sofa. A phone scan of the actual couch, or a bought
scanned asset, is the only way to close the remaining gap; the transform below
is all that a swap would need.
